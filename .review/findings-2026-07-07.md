# Deep Review Findings — quarto-plus — 2026-07-07 (pass 2)

Reviewed at `main` (be6ac44, clean tree). Two-pass mode: addendum of 2026-07-07
governs. Empirical verification performed with Quarto 1.9.38 / pandoc (homebrew):
seven test renders (single-file, hostile-config, many-caption, book project,
default project with subdirectory) with the produced `document.xml` inspected in
each case. All test artifacts were created in the session scratchpad; no source
files were modified.

---

## 1. Architecture summary

quarto-plus is a flat pandoc-Lua plugin: two shortcode modules (`style.lua`,
`crossref.lua`) and three filters (`header.lua`, `table_of_contents.lua`,
`terms_and_abbreviations.lua`) registered in
`_extensions/quarto-plus/_extension.yml`, sharing only `utils.lua`
(`escape_xml`). Everything emits raw OOXML that delegates final computation to
Word field codes (`TOC`, `SEQ`, `REF`), plus a Meta-driven glossary expander.

**Repo-map spot-check: passed.** All six modules exist with the claimed
responsibilities and line ranges; `_extension.yml` registers exactly the listed
shortcodes and filters; `utils.lua` is the only shared dependency (required by
`crossref.lua:3` and `style.lua:1`). One `(?)` item is now resolved: module
state *does* span chapters in a book render (see flow 3) — books are a single
pandoc invocation.

**Flows traced (empirically, not just read):**

1. *Caption → crossref → docx.* `tbl_caption`/`fig_caption` emit a
   caption paragraph containing `bookmarkStart`, literal label text, a
   `SEQ` field (cached value = Lua counter, `w:dirty="true"`), `bookmarkEnd`,
   then the caption text; the id maps record bookmark → counter.
   `crossref` guesses Figure/Table from the bookmark-name *prefix*, looks up
   the counter, and emits a `REF <name> \h` field whose cached text is
   `"Label N"` — with **no** `w:dirty`. Verified in rendered XML.
2. *Author error path.* Placeholder-prose injection (`style.lua:7`,
   `crossref.lua:15,68`) is silent; unresolved crossrefs warn and cache
   `"Table ??"`/`"Unknown ??"`; a missing abbreviations file warns and skips;
   an unwritable unmatched-log warns, "errors" (see below), and the render
   still completes; two other author-error paths abort the render outright
   (finding F3).
3. *Book render.* One pandoc invocation over the merged book: counters,
   id maps, and `seenAcronyms` correctly span chapters — cross-chapter
   crossrefs resolve, first-use glossary expansion happens exactly once
   document-wide, bookmark numbering does not restart. Book projects work.

**Priority-target verdicts** (each treated as a hypothesis):

- Target 1 (crossref dual authority): **confirmed, with one refinement.**
  The blanket claim "forward references resolve to `??`" is **partially
  refuted**: an *inline* forward reference resolves correctly, because Quarto
  expands block-context shortcodes (captions in their own paragraphs) before
  inline-context ones. A crossref *alone in its own paragraph* is
  block-context and does fail forward (`"Table ??"` cached). See F1.
- Target 2 (fail-soft consistency): **the hard-abort claim is refuted.**
  Quarto's filter runtime overrides the global `error()` with a pure stderr
  logger (`/Applications/quarto/share/filters/main.lua:2615-2617`), so
  `terms_and_abbreviations.lua:33` logs and *continues* — verified with an
  unwritable log path: `Output created` still printed. The consistency and
  visibility problems are real (F7, F8), and two *different* paths do
  hard-abort (F3).
- Target 3 (gls four-way duplication/drift): **confirmed** (F6).
- Target 4 (book projects): **largely refuted as a risk** — books work
  (flow 3). The real path problem is elsewhere: subdirectory documents in a
  *default* project silently lose the whole glossary (F4).
- Target 5 (OOXML trust boundary): **confirmed and worse than surveyed** —
  a style map containing `&` produces a non-well-formed `document.xml`,
  i.e. a corrupt .docx (F2); bookmark-name grammar is unvalidated (F9).

**Structural health.** The Word-field-delegation design is sound and the
module boundaries are clean. The weaknesses are concentrated at exactly two
seams: (a) the crossref *cached text* is a second numbering authority that is
never refreshed, and (b) author-controlled strings cross into OOXML/field-code
syntax with escaping applied at some sites and not others. Both are fixable
without restructuring.

## 2. Primary findings (ranked)

### F1. Crossref resolution is position- and paragraph-context-dependent, and wrong results are cached permanently because `REF` is the only field emitted without `w:dirty`

**Severity:** High — **Category:** A (structural) / D — **Confidence:** High (empirical)

**Evidence:**
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/crossref.lua:145-161` — `REF` field emitted with a
  plain `begin` fldChar (`:147`), no `w:dirty`; cached text `"Label N"`.
- Every other field in the codebase is dirty: `crossref.lua:41,94` (SEQ),
  `table_of_contents.lua:46,72,99` (TOC).
- `crossref.lua:128-138` — the label and the map to consult are guessed from
  the bookmark-name prefix (`^[Ff]igure|^[Ff]ig`, `^[Tt]able|^[Tt]bl`), not
  from which caption shortcode registered the bookmark.
- Empirical (t1/t5 renders, `document.xml` inspected):
  - inline forward ref → cached `'Table 2'` (works);
  - crossref alone in its own paragraph, forward → cached `'Table ??'`;
  - `{{< crossref "MyPlot" >}}` where `{{< fig_caption "MyPlot" ... >}}`
    *exists* and its bookmark is present → cached `'Unknown ??'`;
  - none of these carry `w:dirty`.

**The issue.** Display numbers come from live `SEQ` fields (Word recomputes
them on open, since they are dirty), but crossref text comes from Lua
encounter-order counters snapshotted at shortcode-expansion time. Whether the
snapshot is right depends on Quarto's shortcode expansion order: block-context
shortcodes expand before inline-context ones, so the same
`{{< crossref "Table 2" >}}` works inside a sentence but fails in its own
paragraph. And when the snapshot is wrong (`"Table ??"`, `"Unknown ??"`), the
missing `w:dirty` means Word never recomputes the field on open; the `REF`
would resolve correctly on F9-update (the bookmark exists in both failing
cases), but nothing triggers that. The prefix-guessing adds a second failure
mode that is pure convention: any caption whose bookmark id doesn't start with
Figure/Fig/Table/Tbl is reported "Unknown" *even though the extension itself
created and registered it*.

**Blast radius.** Delivered client-facing Word documents containing
`Table ??` / `Unknown ??` in running text. Fires whenever an author (a) puts a
crossref in its own paragraph before the caption, (b) names a bookmark
unconventionally, or (c) any future Quarto version changes shortcode expansion
order — at which point *all* inline forward references break at once, silently.

**Remediation sketch** (hours):
1. Add `w:dirty="true"` to the `REF` begin fldChar (`crossref.lua:147`) — one
   line; Word then refreshes every crossref on open, making the cached text
   advisory rather than authoritative.
2. Replace the two maps + prefix guessing with one map
   `bookmark_id -> {label, n}` written by both caption shortcodes and read by
   `crossref` — kills the "Unknown" class entirely and makes the label come
   from the caption that actually owns the bookmark.
3. Document (README) that a crossref preceding its caption may show `??` until
   fields are updated, or keep a "fields updated on open" guarantee via (1).

### F2. `toc-style-map` values are interpolated into the TOC field instruction unescaped — an `&` or `<` in a style name produces a non-well-formed `document.xml` (corrupt .docx)

**Severity:** High — **Category:** D (trust boundary) — **Confidence:** High (empirical)

**Evidence:**
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/table_of_contents.lua:22-31` — style/level pairs
  concatenated raw; `:6-14` — `build_toc_instruction` string.formats them into
  the instruction; `:96-114` — instruction placed in `<w:instrText>` with no
  `utils.escape_xml` (contrast `crossref.lua:61,114,161` and `style.lua:22`,
  which escape every interpolation).
- Empirical (t3): frontmatter style `"Plain & Simple <Style>"` yields
  instrText content `TOC \o "1-3" \h \z \t "My “Fancy” Style,1,Plain & Simple ,2"`
  and `xml.dom.minidom` rejects the produced `word/document.xml`
  (`not well-formed (invalid token): line 15, column 108` — the bare `&`).
  The render reports success; the corruption is discovered by the recipient.
- Same test shows two silent value-garbling effects of round-tripping config
  through markdown: straight quotes become curly quotes (`My “Fancy” Style` —
  will never match a Word style name, and curly quotes inside a `\t` argument
  are not field-syntax quotes), and `<Style>` is dropped entirely by
  `pandoc.utils.stringify` (raw-HTML inline).

**The issue.** One emitting site skipped the escaping discipline the rest of
the codebase follows. Because pandoc passes `RawBlock('openxml', ...)` content
through verbatim, invalid XML flows straight into the package.

**Blast radius.** A single `&` in a configured TOC style name (plausible in
Word style names) makes the entire rendered document unopenable, with a
successful render log. Quotes/commas in style names silently produce a TOC
`\t` switch Word misparses (comma is also the pair separator).

**Remediation sketch** (hours): apply `utils.escape_xml` to the built
instruction at the emission site; additionally validate each style/level pair
(reject or warn on `,`/`"` and non-numeric levels) at `:25-29`, and warn when
a style name arrives visibly garbled (curly quotes). Consider documenting that
`\u` is dropped when a style map is supplied (`:8-13`) — currently an
undocumented behavior change.

### F3. Two author-typo paths hard-abort the render through unprotected pandoc API calls — direct violations of the stated fail-soft policy

**Severity:** High — **Category:** B (error handling) — **Confidence:** High (empirical)

**Evidence:**
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/terms_and_abbreviations.lua:101,118,135,154` —
  fallback `pandoc.read(replacement...).blocks[1].content`. For `\gls{}`
  (empty braces) `replacement` is `""`, `pandoc.read("").blocks` is empty, and
  indexing nil aborts. Empirical (t2): render fails with
  `terms_and_abbreviations.lua:135: attempt to index a nil value (field 'integer index')`.
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/table_of_contents.lua:26-27` —
  `pandoc.utils.stringify(entry.style)` / `entry.level` with a missing key.
  Empirical (t6, a `toc-style-map` entry with `style` but no `level`): render
  aborts with `Cannot get Attr from TypeNil` at `table_of_contents.lua:27`.

**The issue.** The fail-soft policy [addendum Q3: "a shortcode failure must
never fail the render"] is enforced by `or`-defaults and warnings on the
*anticipated* paths, but the unanticipated ones call pandoc C-API functions
that raise real Lua errors, which Quarto's harness does not catch (unlike the
neutered global `error()` — see F7). Both triggers are one-character author
mistakes: an empty `\gls{}` left as a placeholder while drafting; a YAML
indentation slip in `toc-style-map`.

**Blast radius.** Whole-render failure with a Quarto-internals stack trace
that never names the author's actual mistake (the t2 trace points at
`main.lua` and a walk internal before the extension file). In a template used
across many reports, one glossary typo blocks a document team.

**Remediation sketch** (hours): in `terms_and_abbreviations.lua`, guard the
fallback (`if replacement == "" then return r end`, or check
`#parsed.blocks > 0` before indexing — one shared helper once F6's
deduplication lands); in `table_of_contents.lua`, validate `entry.style` and
`entry.level` for nil before stringify and warn-and-skip the malformed entry.

### F4. `abbreviations-file` and the unmatched log resolve against pandoc's cwd — a document in a project subdirectory silently loses the entire glossary

**Severity:** High — **Category:** D/E — **Confidence:** High (empirical)

**Evidence:**
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/terms_and_abbreviations.lua:2-3` (cwd-relative
  defaults), `:70-75` (meta overrides, still relative), `:9,24` (`io.open`
  directly on the relative path).
- Commit `04a96f0` ("write to quarto project root") only changed the default
  *string* from `formatting_files/abbreviations.tex` to `abbreviations.tex`;
  nothing anchors it to the project root.
- Empirical: in a default-type project with `abbreviations.tex` at the root,
  `quarto render sub/doc.qmd` warns
  `Abbreviations file not found: abbreviations.tex. Skipping abbreviation processing.`,
  renders "successfully" with every `\gls{PK}` degraded to bare `PK` (no
  first-use definition anywhere in the document), and writes
  `sub/unmatched_glossary_terms.log` listing *every* term as unmatched.
- Contrast: the book-project render (single pandoc invocation from the project
  root) finds the file and behaves correctly — so the same document tree works
  or breaks depending on project type and invocation directory.

**The issue.** Pandoc's cwd is the input document's directory for per-file
renders, so the README-documented layout ("default is `abbreviations.tex` in
the quarto project root", `README.md:65`) only works for root-level documents
and book projects. The failure is fail-soft, which here is the problem: the
render succeeds and the missing expansions are only detectable by reading the
output or noticing a console warning in CI noise.

**Blast radius.** Any multi-document project with reports in subdirectories:
delivered documents whose acronyms are never defined and whose abbreviations
table renders without definitions — exactly the "silent wrong content" class
the addendum prioritizes. Log files scattered into content directories.

**Remediation sketch** (hours): resolve both paths against the project root
when available — `quarto.project.directory` (or `os.getenv("QUARTO_PROJECT_DIR")`)
with fallback to the document directory via `quarto.doc.input_file`; try
project-root then document-relative and warn with the *absolute* paths tried.

### F5. Extension bookmark `w:id`s collide with pandoc's own bookmark ids from the 9th caption onward — spec-invalid duplicate annotation ids in every large report

**Severity:** Medium — **Category:** D (serialization contract) — **Confidence:** High that duplicates are emitted (empirical); Medium that Word visibly misbehaves

**Evidence:**
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/crossref.lua:9,24,77` — `current_bookmark_id`
  starts at 0 and increments per caption; emitted as `w:id` at `:61,114`.
- Empirical (t4, 12 headers + 12 captions): `document.xml` contains
  `w:id="9"` for both pandoc's `header-number-1` bookmark and the extension's
  `Table9`, and likewise ids 10–12 — four duplicated ids in one small
  document. Pandoc's docx writer numbered its header bookmarks from 9 in every
  document tested, so collisions begin at the 9th caption.
- The example render also shows pandoc header bookmarks *enclosing* extension
  bookmarks (pandoc's `crossref.lua` heading bookmark id=13 spans extension
  ids 1–2), so at ≥13 captions a duplicated id pair can be *nested*, making
  start/end pairing genuinely ambiguous.

**The issue.** ECMA-376 pairs `bookmarkStart`/`bookmarkEnd` by `w:id`, which
must be unique among the document's annotations; two independent counters
share one id space with no mechanism keeping them apart. Duplicated —
especially nested-duplicated — ids risk mis-paired ranges, "unreadable
content" repair prompts, or silently wrong `REF`/hyperlink ranges, varying by
Word version. Clinical reports routinely exceed 8 captions, so this is the
steady state, not an edge case.

**Blast radius.** Every real-sized report emits spec-invalid XML today; if a
Word/LibreOffice version enforces pairing strictly, the failure lands in
delivered documents and will be near-impossible to trace back.

**Remediation sketch** (hours): offset the extension's counter far above
pandoc's range (e.g. start at 100000 — `w:id` is a decimal number, well within
range), or derive ids from a hash of the bookmark name. One-line change plus a
render check.

### F6. `\gls`-family expansion is four copy-pasted branches whose logs and comments describe transformations the code does not perform

**Severity:** Medium — **Category:** A (duplication with drift) — **Confidence:** High

**Evidence:** `_extensions/quarto-plus/terms_and_abbreviations.lua:83-158`,
branches at `:86` (`\Glspl`), `:104` (`\glspl`), `:121` (`\gls`), `:138`
(`\Gls`). Drift already present:
- `:110` logs `expansion[1]:lower() .. "s"` and `:111` comments "Uncapitalize
  the content", but `:112` inserts `expansion[1]` un-lowered.
- `:127` logs `expansion[1]:lower()`; `:129` inserts `expansion[1]` as-is.
- `:144-145` log `sub(1,1):upper() .. sub(2):lower()` (rest lowercased);
  `:147-148` insert `sub(1,1):upper() .. sub(2)` (rest untouched).
- Each branch re-implements the same five steps (match, lookup, first-use
  bookkeeping, unmatched bookkeeping, fallback) with only the case/plural
  decoration differing; the unguarded fallback of F3 is likewise repeated
  four times, so any fix must be applied in four places or drift further.

**The issue.** For any expansion whose stored definition contains capitals
(proper nouns, trade names), the debug log shows different text than the
document contains, which will burn exactly the person debugging a glossary
discrepancy (`README.md:77-85` documents the intended per-command behavior).
The duplication is the mechanism by which the logs drifted and by which future
edits will drift further.

**Blast radius.** Maintenance tax on the most-edited module; misleading logs
during glossary QC; four sites for every bug fix (see F3).

**Remediation sketch** (hours): one
`expand(key, {capitalize=bool, pluralize=bool})` function; the dispatcher
becomes a 4-entry table `{["\\Glspl"]={true,true}, ...}` checked longest-prefix
first. Log the exact string that is inserted.

### F7. `error()` inside Quarto filters is a no-op logger, so the abort intent at the log-write site silently doesn't hold — and the unmatched-log lifecycle reports files it didn't write and preserves stale logs from prior renders

**Severity:** Medium — **Category:** B — **Confidence:** High (empirical)

**Evidence:**
- Quarto's filter runtime redefines the global `error(message, offset)` as
  `io.stderr:write(...)` — it does not raise
  (`/Applications/quarto/share/filters/main.lua:2615-2617`, Quarto 1.9.38).
- `_extensions/quarto-plus/terms_and_abbreviations.lua:32-34` — warning +
  `error()` written as an abort. Empirical (t7,
  `unmatched-glossary-log: /nonexistent-dir-xyz/terms.log`): the render prints
  the warning, the red ERROR line, **then**
  `(W) Found 1 unmatched glossary term(s). See: /nonexistent-dir-xyz/terms.log`
  and `Output created` — pointing the author at a log that was never written.
- `:190-193` — the log is written only when `#unmatchedTerms > 0` and never
  removed otherwise (commit `bacf039` "only write if n > 0"), so a log from a
  previous render survives after the author fixes every term: a stale file
  asserting unmatched terms that no longer exist.

**The issue.** Three compounding visibility defects on the one channel meant
to make glossary failures auditable: the abort semantics the code appears to
have don't exist under Quarto (and *would* fire under plain
`pandoc --lua-filter`, giving the same file two behaviors in two hosts); the
"See: <file>" message is emitted regardless of write success; and the file's
absence-vs-staleness carries no meaning between renders.

**Blast radius.** QC workflows keyed off the unmatched log (its whole purpose)
can be misled in both directions — told to read a nonexistent file, or acting
on last week's failures.

**Remediation sketch** (hours): make `writeLogFile` return success; only print
the "See:" message on success; delete (or truncate) the log at the start of
the `Pandoc` pass so absence always means "no unmatched terms this render";
drop the misleading `error()` or replace it with an explicit
`quarto.log.error` so the intent is unambiguous.

### F8. Fail-soft placeholder prose is injected into deliverables with no warning, while comparable failures elsewhere warn — the policy exists but has no consistent surface

**Severity:** Medium — **Category:** B — **Confidence:** High

**Evidence — the author-error table across the five modules:**

| Path | Behavior | Console signal |
|---|---|---|
| `style.lua:7` missing text arg | injects "If you see this, you did not provide text..." | none |
| `style.lua:6` missing style arg | silently styles as `Normal` | none |
| `crossref.lua:15,68` missing caption text | injects "If you see this, you did not provide caption text." | none |
| `crossref.lua:14,67,120` missing bookmark arg | silently uses `defaultBookId`; captions overwrite each other's map entry | none |
| `crossref.lua:137,141` unknown prefix / unresolved | injects `Unknown ??` / `Table ??` | warning |
| `terms:16` missing abbreviations file | all terms degrade to bare keys | warning |
| `terms:97` (etc.) unmatched term | bare key in output | warning + log file |
| `terms:33` log unwritable | log lost | warning + inert ERROR (F7) |
| `terms:101` (etc.) empty `\gls{}`; `toc:27` bad map entry | **render aborts** | stack trace (F3) |

**The issue.** The same policy ("never fail the render") produces four
different author experiences: silent placeholder in the deliverable, warning,
warning+file, and (accidentally) abort. The silent rows are the dangerous
ones: placeholder sentences and `defaultBookId` bookmarks ship to clients with
a completely clean render log, and the two caption shortcodes sharing
`defaultBookId` means two argless captions silently cross-wire each other's
references.

**Blast radius.** Placeholder prose in delivered documents is the exact
failure class the maintainers named as the review's top concern; today it is
the *least* observable one.

**Remediation sketch** (hours): add `quarto.log.warning` at every injection
site (`style.lua:6-7`, `crossref.lua:14-15,67-68`); make the placeholder texts
include the shortcode name; consider a distinctive marker string (e.g.
`[[quarto-plus: missing caption text]]`) that QC can grep rendered text for.

### F9. Bookmark names are whitespace-stripped but never validated against Word's bookmark-name grammar

**Severity:** Medium — **Category:** D — **Confidence:** Medium (grammar per ECMA-376/Word UI; Word-side behavior not exercised)

**Evidence:** `_extensions/quarto-plus/crossref.lua:14,67,120` — the only
normalization is `:gsub("%s+", "")`; the result goes into
`w:bookmarkStart/@w:name` (`:61,114`) and the `REF` instruction (`:150,161`).
Word bookmark names must start with a letter and contain only letters, digits,
and underscores, max 40 chars. Authors following the README's own naming style
(`"Table 1"`) are safe only because the space is stripped; `"Table-1a"`,
`"Fig.2"`, `"3rdCohort"`, or a >40-char descriptive id all pass through
unchecked. XML-escaping (`:61`) keeps the XML well-formed, so the failure
surfaces only inside Word — as a dropped bookmark and a `REF` that yields
"Error! Reference source not found." on update, i.e. at the client's desk.

**Blast radius.** Same silent-wrong-deliverable class as F1, triggered purely
by id spelling; interacts with F1's missing `w:dirty` (the bad cached text is
what actually ships until fields update).

**Remediation sketch** (hours): a shared `sanitize_bookmark_name()` in
`utils.lua` used by all three shortcodes — replace illegal characters
deterministically (non-`[%w_]` → `_`, prefix if the first char is not a
letter, truncate to 40), warn when the name was altered; using it at both the
caption and crossref sites keeps the two sides agreeing by construction.

## 3. Low-hanging fruit

- `header.lua:16` — the `el.content[1].text == ""` arm is dead: pandoc never
  produces an empty `Str`, and a non-`Str` first inline gives `nil ~= ""`.
  Simplify to `#el.content == 0`.
- `crossref.lua:146` — `xmlns:w` declared on this one `w:r` and nowhere else
  in the codebase's raw OOXML; pandoc splices it into a document whose root
  already declares the namespace. Delete for consistency.
- `table_of_contents.lua:8-13` — supplying `toc-style-map` silently drops the
  `\u` switch; not mentioned in `README.md:35-45`. Document or retain `\u`.
- `table_of_contents.lua:20` — `toc-levels: 3` (a bare number) yields
  `TOC \o "3"`, not a valid Word heading range (`"1-3"`); validate the
  `%d+-%d+` shape and warn.
- `terms_and_abbreviations.lua:99,116,133,152` — `unmatchedTerms` collects one
  entry per *use*; the log lists the same term repeatedly. De-duplicate.
- `crossref.lua:23,76` — re-using a bookmark id in a second caption silently
  overwrites the map entry and emits two `bookmarkStart` elements with the
  same `w:name` (invalid in Word); warn on duplicate registration.
- `terms_and_abbreviations.lua:175-182` — the `.abbreviations` table is built
  as a markdown pipe table; a definition containing `|` corrupts it. Escape
  `|` when concatenating.

## 4. Questions for maintainers

1. **Literal tab in headings** (`header.lua:36-37`): the tab survives to
   `document.xml` as a raw `\t` inside `<w:t xml:space="preserve">` (verified)
   rather than a `<w:tab/>` element. Desktop Word evidently renders this as
   you intend, but it is not the canonical representation, and other consumers
   (LibreOffice, docx→PDF services, python-docx post-processing) may drop or
   collapse it. Would emitting `<w:tab/>` via a raw inline be acceptable?
2. **Caption text is plain-text only**: `tbl_caption`/`fig_caption` pass the
   caption through `escape_xml` into a single `w:t`, so markdown in captions
   (italic species names, sub/superscripts — common in PK/PD) is impossible.
   Known limitation, or worth a `pandoc.read` path like the glossary uses?
3. **Is `quarto render` of a default-type project with documents in
   subdirectories a supported workflow?** If yes, F4 is urgent; if the
   report template constrains documents to the project root, F4 drops to
   Medium.
4. **The `REF` cached-text format** `"Label N"` matches what Word produces on
   field update only because the caption bookmark deliberately spans
   `Label <SEQ>` (`crossref.lua:33-55`). Is that pairing documented anywhere?
   It is load-bearing for F1's remediation.

## 5. What's good

- **Delegating numbering to Word fields is the right architecture.** SEQ +
  bookmarked caption + `TOC \c` interlock correctly (verified in output), the
  only cross-module contract (the `Figure`/`Table` SEQ identifiers shared by
  `crossref.lua:44,97` and `table_of_contents.lua:49,75`) holds, and books
  work unmodified because everything rides a single pandoc invocation.
  Preserve this; fix the cached-text layer on top of it (F1), not the field
  design.
- **The staged filter-table pattern** in `terms_and_abbreviations.lua`
  (Meta → RawInline → Div → Pandoc as four separate passes) makes the temporal
  coupling explicit and correct — the abbreviations div can appear *before*
  first use and still lists all terms (verified in the book render).
- **`utils.escape_xml` discipline** is applied at six of seven interpolation
  sites through a single shared helper; F2 is the one hole, not a systemic
  absence. Pervasive `quarto.log.debug` made every empirical trace in this
  review easy to follow.
